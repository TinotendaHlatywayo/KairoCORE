# Manage staff accounts and permissions

Access control has two layers: **user accounts** (who can sign in) and **roles** (what they can see and do). Both live under **System Administration → User Management**.

## 1. Create staff accounts

Open **System Administration → User Management → User Accounts** and add a user for each staff member: name, email, and the role(s) they should hold. The user receives an activation email and sets their own password.

Staff records created through **HR & Payroll → Employees** also provision a linked portal account automatically.

## 2. Choose from built-in roles

The platform ships sensible defaults (System Administrator, School Administrator, Teaching Staff, Accounts / Finance, HR, Health, Procurement, Librarian, Houseparent, Supporting Staff). Attach one or more to a user; their sidebar and abilities update to match.

## 3. Build a custom role

When a job needs exactly one slice of access:

1. Open **System Administration → User Management → Roles**.
2. Create a new role and name it after the job (for example "Grade 7 Coordinator").
3. Tick only the resources and actions this role needs. Save, then assign the role to the user.

Custom roles are the right tool when a staff member should touch one area deeply but never see another.

## 4. Departments

Open **System Administration → User Management → Departments** to describe your staff structure. Departments group employees for payroll filtering, reporting and internal ownership of records.

## Principles that keep schools safe

- **Least privilege** — grant what the job needs, nothing more.
- **Separate money from academics** — bursars get finance; teachers get marks; few people need both fully.
- **Review on departure** — deactivate the account the same day someone leaves; do not delete users who have historical records attached.

For an audit trail of who changed what, use **System Administration → Audit & Compliance → Audit Log**.
